Four findings matter for the model.

1. The Italian zero after month 24 is the OECD's published value and it follows the rules. TaxBEN 2022 gives 25% from month 25 for a single person claiming social assistance, and from 2023 it gives zero. From 2024 the reason is documented: the Assegno di inclusione excludes households without a minor, a disabled person or a person aged 60 or more. For 2023 the likely reason is the budget law that capped the Reddito di cittadinanza at seven monthly payments for the same households. This settles the open item in `audit_4_data_place.md`. With 2022 rules the Italian `rr` would be 0.444, close to the auditor's guess of about 0.45.
2. In Germany the standard rate of the Bürgergeld is already inside the replacement rate. TaxBEN classifies it as unemployment assistance, so the German profile is 0.59 for twelve months and 0.16 afterwards whether social assistance is switched on or off. Adding a floor on top of the current `rr` would count the German minimum income twice.
3. TaxBEN pays the French insurance benefit for 24 months. The rule in force since 1 February 2023 is 548 days (about 18 months) under age 53. With 18 months the French single rate falls from 0.653 to 0.625.
4. The household-weighted rate is higher than the single rate mainly because a working partner's earnings stay in the household. That part is private insurance. The state pays about 0.48 (FR), 0.36 (DE) and 0.29 (IT) of previous household income, and only this part should enter the model's financing.

- France 2025: 12 x 635.71 (RSA for a single person at 1 January 2025) + 152.45 (prime de Noël) = 7,780.97, the OECD's 7,781. The RSA is 646.52 a month from April 2025 (MISSOC) and 651.69 from April 2026 (service-public), and 977.54 for a couple.
- Germany: 12 x 502 = 6,024 in 2023 and 12 x 563 = 6,756 in 2024 and 2025. For a couple 24 x 451 = 10,824 and 24 x 506 = 12,144. Housing in TaxBEN is the Berlin ceiling, 426 rent plus 69.50 heating a month for one person (12 x 495.50 = 5,946 = 12,702 - 6,756) and 515.45 plus 90.35 for two.

### 3.2 Italy

The Assegno di inclusione (ADI), in force since 1 January 2024, is paid only to households that include a minor, a person aged 60 or more, a disabled person or a disadvantaged member (OECD policy description, section 3.1.1, and MISSOC). A single adult or a childless couple of working age who can work receives nothing from it, which is why TaxBEN reports zero. For eligible households the annual amount from 2025 is max(0, 6,500 x scale + min(rent, 3,640) - household income), up from 6,000 and 3,360 in 2024. It runs for 18 months and can be renewed for 12 months at a time.

Adults aged 18 to 59 who can work may claim the Supporto per la formazione e il lavoro: EUR 500 a month from 2025 (350 before), for 12 months, extendable by at most 12, and paid only while the person attends a training or activation programme. The OECD treats it as a training allowance and leaves it out of TaxBEN. As a share of gross AW it would be 6,000 / 36,594 = 0.164 in 2025, but it is time-limited and conditional, so in my view it is not a consumption floor.

For 2023 the two OECD dataflows disagree for the childless household: the adequacy flow shows the full Reddito di cittadinanza (6,000, or 9,360 with rent), and the replacement-rate flow shows zero after month 24. The seven-payment cap of the 2023 budget law (Camera dei deputati dossier) is the likely reason. The OECD text I read is the 2025 edition and does not say so, hence this explanation is my inference (UNVERIFIED as to cause).

### 3.4 In the model's terms

The floor is the level c_bar in the transfer TR = max(0, c_bar - cash on hand) of Hubbard, Skinner and Zeldes (1995). I checked the working-paper version (NBER 4884): their floor adds housing assistance to cash assistance and food stamps, so the "housing yes" rows are the closer counterpart. The floor is in the same unit as the wage. If model income is net, as the replacement rate is, the column to use is the share of net AW. If the model computes a median income, the OECD indicator (36 to 37% of median disposable income in France and Germany) maps directly and sits below the model's poverty line at 50% of the median.

The asset test differs by country, and the Hubbard, Skinner and Zeldes form fits Germany and Italy better than France. In France the benefit falls by 3% of assets a year, so a household keeps most of the RSA at any plausible level of liquid wealth, and the French floor behaves as an income floor. In Germany and Italy eligibility stops at a level of financial wealth of roughly 0.25 and 0.16 to 0.18 of the average wage per adult.

The long-term shares implied by `lfsa_ugad` for ages 15 to 74 in 2023 are 0.245, 0.311 and 0.560, the model's values.

In the model's terms this is the share of the unemployed for whom the TaxBEN profile does not apply at all, since TaxBEN assumes a long contribution record and full take-up. The German pattern, with more coverage among the long-term unemployed, reflects the Bürgergeld. In France and Italy the long-term unemployed are covered less than the short-term. The figures are self-reported, and which national benefits respondents count as "benefit or assistance" is not documented in the table (UNVERIFIED: whether the French RSA is counted).

A coverage-adjusted rate would multiply the insurance phase by the share covered among the short-term unemployed aged 25 to 64 (FR 0.565, DE 0.651, IT 0.361). For a single person at 100% AW that gives initial rates of 0.68 x 0.565 = 0.38, 0.59 x 0.651 = 0.38 and 0.63 x 0.361 = 0.23. It changes the object from the entitlement of an insured worker to the average over insured and uninsured, so I report it as a sensitivity and do not recommend it as the baseline.

## 7. Recommendation

### 7.1 Floor

Use the single adult with housing support, in the unit of the model's income.

A floor for the Italian households that do qualify for the ADI needs the share of eligible households, which I did not find in a published table.

### 7.2 Household-weighted replacement rate

Weights are the three childless types from section 5. Singles are the one-adult classes, and couples and other households are split by the probability that the partner works. Rates are TaxBEN 2023, 100% AW, social assistance yes, housing benefit no.

France. Singles 0.183 + 0.079 = 0.262. Two-earner (0.134 + 0.172) x 0.452 + (0.316 + 0.116) x 0.754 = 0.138 + 0.326 = 0.464. One-earner 1 - 0.262 - 0.464 = 0.274.
rr = 0.262 x 0.653 + 0.464 x 0.781 + 0.274 x 0.621 = 0.171 + 0.362 + 0.170 = 0.704.

Germany. Singles 0.219 + 0.066 = 0.284 (unrounded). Two-earner (0.117 + 0.176) x 0.582 + (0.327 + 0.095) x 0.724 = 0.171 + 0.305 = 0.476. One-earner 0.239.
rr = 0.284 x 0.456 + 0.476 x 0.711 + 0.239 x 0.487 = 0.130 + 0.338 + 0.117 = 0.585.

Italy. Singles 0.096 + 0.025 = 0.121. Two-earner (0.076 + 0.381) x 0.438 + (0.288 + 0.135) x 0.768 = 0.200 + 0.325 = 0.525. One-earner 0.355.
rr = 0.121 x 0.374 + 0.525 x 0.650 + 0.355 x 0.397 = 0.045 + 0.341 + 0.141 = 0.527.

Against the current 0.653, 0.456 and 0.374, the weighted rates are 0.704, 0.585 and 0.527. At 67% AW they are 0.718, 0.628 and 0.581.

Two conditions apply to their use.

- Financing. The model taxes to pay rr x wage. With the weighted rate that would charge the state for the partner's earnings. The part the state pays is 0.704 - 0.464 x 0.49 = 0.476 in France, 0.585 - 0.476 x 0.47 = 0.361 in Germany and 0.527 - 0.525 x 0.45 = 0.291 in Italy. The household's income when unemployed should use the weighted rate and the tax should use the public part.
- With an explicit floor. The floor replaces the assistance already inside the rates of singles and one-earner couples, so the insurance-only weighted rate is the companion value: 0.262 x 0.639 + 0.464 x 0.781 + 0.274 x 0.601 = 0.695 in France, 0.284 x 0.407 + 0.476 x 0.711 + 0.239 x 0.407 = 0.552 in Germany, and 0.527 in Italy, where nothing changes.

If the household composition is not adopted, the smaller correction is to keep the single type and drop the assistance tail when the floor is introduced: 0.639, 0.407 and 0.374.

## 8. Limits and unverified items

- UNVERIFIED (not published): the unemployed by household type. The weights use the not employed aged 25 to 54, which includes the inactive, and the partner's status has no age split. Inactive partners in couples are numerous in Italy, so the Italian couple weights are probably too high for the unemployed. Unemployed-specific weights need EU-LFS or EU-SILC microdata.
- UNVERIFIED (cause): why the two OECD flows differ for the Italian childless household in 2023. The values themselves are the OECD's.
- UNVERIFIED (detail): the commencement date of the new § 12 SGB II. The consolidated text consulted on 2 October 2026 shows the age-based allowances, and the header of the code gives 1 July 2026 for the act of 16 April 2026. I did not read the act in the Bundesgesetzblatt, and the header cites two gazette numbers (107 and 195).
- UNVERIFIED (composition): the average payment entitlement of a one-person benefit unit, EUR 1,029 a month in 2024 (Bundestag Drucksache 21/1069). I did not check which components it includes, so it is in the csv for reference only.
- UNVERIFIED: whether the Reddito di cittadinanza had the same EUR 6,000 financial-asset threshold as the ADI. The table in 3.3 is the ADI rule.
- MISSOC's line for Italy gives EUR 6,500 as the minimum income of a single unemployed person aged 35 under the training allowance. The ministry and the OECD give EUR 500 a month for at most 12 months plus 12. I use the ministry's figure.
- The French RSA amounts for 2023 and 2024 are read from the OECD annual totals. I verified the monthly rate at source only for 2025 and 2026.
- TaxBEN is a model household: age 40, long contribution record, full take-up, no assets above the exemption, rent at 20% of AW (Berlin ceilings in Germany). The replacement rates are rounded to whole percentage points by the OECD, so spell averages carry an error of up to about 0.005.
- Children in the six-class variant are the TaxBEN case of two children aged 4 and 6, applied to every household with children.
- The public part and the insurance-only rates are my constructions from the published profiles and not OECD series.
